ENTRY 01 / REFLECTIONS

Before I felt like I belonged

Noluthando Basi · 10 November 2026 · 2 min read

At first, I thought belonging would feel like certainty. I imagined that finding my place would mean knowing what to say, understanding things quickly, and feeling comfortable calling myself an engineer in the making.

Instead, I found myself wondering whether I fit. There is a particular kind of doubt that comes with being a beginner: you see what other people can do, but you know every gap in your own understanding. It becomes easy to mistake those gaps for proof that you are in the wrong place.

I am a writer, too. Words give me a way to sit with questions I cannot answer immediately. Writing about uncertainty helps me notice the difference between not knowing something yet and not being capable of learning it.

I do not need to know everything before I give myself permission to begin.

That is the thought I want to carry into the things I build. A circuit, a program, a page of writing: each starts with something unfinished. Each asks for attention, patience, and the willingness to try again.

I am learning to measure progress in smaller ways. A concept that makes a little more sense. A question I am willing to ask. The moment I can explain something in my own words. None of these has to be impressive to someone else to matter to me.

I do not have a neat ending where all the doubt disappears. But I am beginning to leave room for another possibility: perhaps I can feel uncertain and still belong here.

This journal is a place for that unfinished story. The builds and the bugs, the lessons and the questions, and the person learning through all of them.

— Noluthando

ENTRY 02 / BUILD NOTES

Two buttons, one awkward question

Noluthando Basi · 1 min read

Our one-bit arithmetic circuit had two mode buttons, one for addition and one for subtraction. We used a latch so the circuit could remember which mode was selected after you let go. That worked neatly when you pressed one button at a time. But what if you pressed both?

In the design review, that turned out to be an invalid state for the latch. The circuit also needed a deliberate mode selection at startup. The arithmetic tests could tell us whether the circuit gave the right answer in a chosen mode. They couldn't answer every question about how it got into that mode.

That changed the questions I'd ask about the next version. What state should it start in? What should happen when two controls disagree? I'd put those cases in the test plan alongside the arithmetic. Two buttons looked like the simplest part of the circuit. They gave me some of the most useful questions.
